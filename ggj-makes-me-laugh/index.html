Concept

Introduction

In this game, the player takes on the role of a professional "Perfect Smile Repair Technician," helping a live-streaming anchor maintain her perfect smile in various scenarios. The game uses keyboard and mouse controls, where the player manages both the anchor's facial expressions and the live-stream interface, including comment moderation. Correct operations will increase the viewer count, while mistakes will lead to a decline. The goal is to keep the viewer count positive and last until the countdown ends to win the game.

Team

Role

In this collaboration, most members, including myself, participated in a game jam for the first time. We brainstormed gameplay ideas together, and my concept received the most votes, making me the lead designer. Additionally, I took on the role of the main artist, responsible for all art assets except for the gift icons.

Gameplay

The theme for the jam was "Make Me Laugh." We explored the idea of "making" smiles, designing a scenario where smiles are essential—in this case, a live-streaming session. The gameplay was inspired by the streaming mechanics in games like Who's Lila and Needy Streamer Overload. Players must control the anchor's facial expressions and manage live-stream comments as core mechanics.


Initially, we intended to implement dynamic facial expressions but faced technical challenges. Drawing from my experience with Live2D, I proposed using a cross-coordinate system to manage expressions. The X and Y axes represent extreme emotions, and the player manipulates the values to adjust the expression. I created 53 unique facial expression illustrations, allowing players to switch between expressions smoothly using the WASD keys.

Art Design

The "peculiar smile" was a core visual theme from the start. My art style was heavily inspired by Rusty Lake, featuring bold, hand-drawn lines to create a distinct and eerie atmosphere. For the interface, I adopted a first-person monitor perspective similar to Do Not Feed the Monkeys, enhancing the immersive experience for players.

Final Thought

This was my first Global Game Jam and my first time participating in a full game development process. Surprisingly, I adapted quickly and found working on the art aspect within the team very enjoyable. Even when we encountered challenges with expression control, I believed that as long as the issue could be resolved through art assets, it wasn’t truly a problem.
